"How much does a website cost?" usually gets answered with "it depends on the
requirements" — true, and useless. Here are the numbers.

## Realistic bands in the Saudi market

Below is for a company site (not a store), in Arabic and English, covering design,
build, and launch. It excludes brand identity and written content:

| Band | What you typically get |
|---|---|
| Under 3,000 SAR | A recoloured template, machine-translated copy, rarely a sound Arabic version |
| 5,000 – 15,000 SAR | A site designed for you, 5–10 pages, a control panel, baseline technical setup |
| 15,000 – 40,000 SAR | Fully custom design, written content, serious SEO groundwork, integrations |
| Over 40,000 SAR | Platforms, multi-branch sites, or complex integrations |

Most small and mid-sized Saudi companies land in the second and third bands.

## What actually raises the cost

Only three things move the number meaningfully:

**The number of *distinct* page designs, not pages.** Ten pages sharing one layout
cost far less than four pages with four layouts. Services, project detail, and team
are three designs — not three copies.

**Who writes the content.** Most delays on website projects trace back to copy the
client promised and didn't send. Arabic content — written, not translated — is a
real line item. Leaving it out of the quote doesn't remove the work; it moves it to
you.

**Integrations.** A CRM, a payment gateway, a booking system, or e-invoicing that
complies with ZATCA's Fatoora — each is a small project inside the project.

## What doesn't raise it, whatever you're told

- **A second language**, if both were planned from the start. What costs money is
  adding the second language after the site was built for the first.
- **"Mobile responsive."** This stopped being a separately priced feature years
  ago. Anyone billing it as an extra is selling you the baseline.
- **Hosting and domain.** Together, under 500 SAR a year for a normal company site.
  Anyone asking thousands annually for "hosting and maintenance" is selling
  something else under that name.

## Where money goes for nothing

**A template at custom-design prices.** Ask to see three previous sites from the
same builder. If the layouts rhyme, you're buying the same template in a new
colour.

**"SEO setup" with no definition.** SEO in a quote should be line items: per-page
titles and descriptions, structured data, a sitemap, Search Console wired up,
measured speed. "Full search engine optimisation" is a phrase, not a deliverable.

**Vague ownership.** The domain, hosting, and accounts must be in your name. If
they're in the agency's, you're renting your own website — and you'll find out the
day you try to leave.

## How to read a quote

Ask three questions before signing:

1. **What happens if I'm late sending content?** A serious answer has a schedule
   and a cut-off, not "no problem."
2. **How many revision rounds are included, and what happens after?** The number
   missing here reappears on the invoice.
3. **What do I own at handover?** The right answer: the files, the control panel,
   the domain, the hosting, and every account.

## In short

A website is an asset, not an expense: if it brings one client a month at a typical
contract value, it pays for itself within months. And the difference between a
5,000 SAR site and a 15,000 SAR one is usually not beauty — it's who wrote the
content, whether it was built to be found, and whether you actually own it.
