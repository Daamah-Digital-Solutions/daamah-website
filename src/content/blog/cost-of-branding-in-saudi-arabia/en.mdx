Plenty of people ask for "a logo" and mean a full brand identity, then are
surprised by the price gap between a logo sketched in a day and a complete
identity system. The difference isn't just skill — it's the scope of the work.

## A logo is not an identity

A logo is one symbol: the mark a company is recognised by. Brand identity is
much wider — colours and their shades, Arabic and Latin typefaces chosen
together, rules for using the logo on different backgrounds, and a guide that
documents all of it so nobody has to reinvent it every time a designer opens a
file.

Pay for a logo alone, then ask every designer who touches your brand
afterwards to "figure out" how it applies to your profile, website, and
social pages, and you pay the difference later — in instalments, and
inconsistently.

## Realistic bands

| Band | What you typically get |
|---|---|
| Under 1,500 SAR | A logo alone, no documented colour system, no real Arabic version |
| 2,500 – 6,000 SAR | Logo + a basic identity: colours, typefaces, applications on stationery and social |
| 6,000 – 15,000 SAR | A full identity system: usage guide, extended applications, Arabic and Latin versions designed together, not translated |
| Over 15,000 SAR | Identity for a multi-division or multi-branch company, with sub-systems per sector |

## What actually raises the price

**Languages designed, not translated.** An Arabic typeface chosen to sit
visually with the Latin one is real design work, not a pick from a default
list.

**Number of applications.** A logo on a business card is one thing; an
animated logo for a video intro is another; a logo applied to a storefront is
a third. Each application is a design decision.

**Revision rounds.** Three considered rounds cost less than ten undirected
ones — and the difference starts before the first meeting, with clear
references brought in advance.

## A warning sign

A designer who hands you a single PNG and calls it "your logo" hasn't
delivered an identity. Always ask for: vector files (AI or SVG) that scale
without losing quality, versions for light and dark backgrounds, and a colour
guide with exact codes (HEX and CMYK) — not a description like "light blue."

## In short

Brand identity is used for years across everything a company produces: the
website, the profile, social media, print. A cheap logo redesigned two years
later ends up costing more than an identity done properly from the start.
