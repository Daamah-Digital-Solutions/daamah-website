Google isn't the only front door anymore. Your prospect now asks ChatGPT or
Google's AI Overview "best web design company in Riyadh" and gets a ready
answer — not a list of links to click through. A company missing from that
answer loses a customer who never used a search engine the old way.

## Why this differs from traditional SEO

A traditional search engine returns links and lets the user decide. An AI
model **reads** your pages, extracts an answer, and quotes or paraphrases it.
Ranking first matters less than having a clear, quotable answer the model can
trust.

## What these models actually look for

**One clear definition of who you are.** A plain paragraph: company X,
founded in Y, offering Z, for clients in these markets. Not marketing slogans
with no facts behind them.

**Direct answers, not build-up.** A page that asks "how much does X cost" and
only answers three paragraphs later gets scanned and skipped. The answer
belongs in the first two sentences.

**Facts stated in a quotable form.** A number, a table, a specific step — not
a general line like "excellent service at competitive prices." Models quote
what can be lifted verbatim and trusted.

**Explicit crawl permission.** A `robots.txt` that blocks AI crawlers
(GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, and others) removes the site from the
equation entirely, regardless of content quality.

## Practical steps you can start today

1. **Check your robots.txt.** Confirm it doesn't block known AI crawlers,
   explicitly or by default.
2. **Write one "about us" paragraph that can be quoted whole.** Not two
   conflicting versions on two different pages.
3. **Turn your FAQ into real questions and answers,** not marketing copy
   split into question-shaped headings.
4. **Add structured data (schema)** describing your business clearly —
   organisation, services, FAQs. Machines trust structured data more than
   free text.
5. **Track citations, not just rankings.** Ask new leads "where did you hear
   about us?" and notice when the answer is "I asked ChatGPT."

## The useful paradox

A page written well for AI models is usually the same page that's best for a
human reader in a hurry: a clear answer up top, specific facts, no padding.
Optimising for AI visibility and optimising for the reader almost always
point the same direction.

## In short

Showing up in AI answers isn't a separate technical trick from SEO — it's a
natural extension of it: more clarity, more direct answers, explicit crawl
permission. Whoever starts now gets ahead of a market where most competitors
haven't yet grasped that the rules changed.
